The three trust-critical overlays

One external-edit event on main.spec.yaml — the common case, a coding agent writing the spec while the workspace is open — can trip all three at once. The spine fixes their precedence: the conflict prompt is modal and wins; the data-loss toast and the drift chip queue behind it and re-check after the reload/keep choice.

1 Conflict prompt — focus-trapped modal

The file changed on disk while edits were pending. Both choices are irreversible, so the prompt is a focus-trapped modal: initial focus sits on the non-destructive Cancel (which writes nothing), Esc maps to it, and the two destructive actions are labeled distinctly in words — never by colour alone. The workspace behind is dimmed; the drift chip and toast wait behind the modal.

2 Sync-health chip — expanded drift popover

Silent when honest, stateful on drift. Here the corner chip is in its drift state (warning-dim fill, warning text, a drift count); focusing it and pressing Enter expands a floating popover of the three MVP detectors — unmapped canvas content, partial-picture, stale positions — each with its own resolve action. The workspace stays live; nothing is dimmed.

3 Data-loss toast — the active nudge

Drift that risks losing work raises a toast on top of the chip. It renders in an aria-live="assertive" region, never steals focus, and keeps its action and dismiss keyboard-reachable. Because a conflict prompt is up in the common case, the toast is shown here in its queued state — held behind the modal and re-checked after the reload/keep choice, and mirrored to the sync entry so it survives the timeout.
